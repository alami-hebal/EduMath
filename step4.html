Matrices & Matrix Operations

Discover the arithmetic of matrices: dimensions, addition, scalar multiplication, the row-column rule for matrix multiplication, transposes, and the trace.

$\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}$

1 Matrix Terminology

A matrix is a rectangular array of numbers. The numbers in the array are called the entries of the matrix. The size of a matrix is described in terms of the number of rows (horizontal) and columns (vertical) it contains, written as $m \times n$.

The entry that occurs in row $i$ and column $j$ of a matrix $A$ is typically denoted by $a_{ij}$.

Column Vector

Size: $m \times 1$

$$ \begin{bmatrix} b_1 \\ b_2 \\ \vdots \\ b_m \end{bmatrix} $$

Row Vector

Size: $1 \times n$

$$ \begin{bmatrix} a_1 & a_2 & \dots & a_n \end{bmatrix} $$

Square Matrix

Size: $n \times n$

$$ \begin{bmatrix} a_{11} & a_{12} \\ a_{21} & a_{22} \end{bmatrix} $$

Main Diagonal

Entries where $i = j$

$$ \begin{bmatrix} \color{blue}{a_{11}} & a_{12} \\ a_{21} & \color{blue}{a_{22}} \end{bmatrix} $$

2 Basic Operations

Addition & Subtraction

If $A$ and $B$ are matrices of the same size, their sum $A+B$ (or difference $A-B$) is obtained by adding (or subtracting) the corresponding entries.

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 3 & 6 \end{bmatrix} $$

Scalar Multiplication

If $A$ is any matrix and $c$ is a scalar (a real number), the product $cA$ is obtained by multiplying every entry of $A$ by $c$.

$$ 3 \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 3 & -4 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & 3 \\ 9 & -12 \end{bmatrix} $$

3 Matrix Multiplication (Row-Column Rule)

Matrix multiplication is not as simple as multiplying corresponding entries. If $A$ is an $m \times r$ matrix and $B$ is an $r \times n$ matrix, the product $AB$ is an $m \times n$ matrix.

⚠️

Crucial Rule: The number of columns in the first matrix must equal the number of rows in the second matrix. The "inside" dimensions must match: $(m \times \mathbf{r}) \cdot (\mathbf{r} \times n) = (m \times n)$.

4 Transpose & Trace

The Transpose ($A^T$)

The transpose of an $m \times n$ matrix $A$ is the $n \times m$ matrix resulting from interchanging the rows and columns of $A$. Row 1 becomes Column 1, Row 2 becomes Column 2, etc.

$$ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \end{bmatrix} \implies A^T = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 4 \\ 2 & 5 \\ 3 & 6 \end{bmatrix} $$

The Trace ($tr(A)$)

If $A$ is a square matrix, the trace of $A$ is defined to be the sum of the entries on the main diagonal. It is undefined for non-square matrices.

$$ A = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{2} & -1 & 4 \\ 3 & \mathbf{5} & 0 \\ -5 & 8 & \mathbf{-3} \end{bmatrix} $$

$tr(A) = 2 + 5 + (-3) = 4$
