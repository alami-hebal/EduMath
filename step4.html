1 What is a Linear Equation?

A linear equation in $n$ variables $x_{1}, x_{2}, \dots, x_{n}$ is an equation that can be written in the standard form:

$$a_{1}x_{1} + a_{2}x_{2} + \dots + a_{n}x_{n} = b$$

Where $a_{1}, \dots, a_{n}$ and $b$ are constants (and the $a$'s are not all zero). Notice that linear equations never contain products of variables (like $xy$), roots, or powers other than $1$.

Examples vs. Non-Examples

  • ✓ $x + 3y = 7$ (Linear)
  • ✓ $\frac{1}{2}x_1 - x_2 + 3x_3 = -1$ (Linear)
  • ✗ $x_1 + 3x_2^{\color{red}2} = 4$ (Not linear - squared variable)
  • ✗ $3x + 2y - {\color{red}xy} = 5$ (Not linear - product of variables)

The Three Types of Solutions

Every system of linear equations has exactly zero, one, or infinitely many solutions. There are no other possibilities. Geometrically, in 2D or 3D, this translates to how lines or planes intersect.

0 Solutions
⏸

Inconsistent System

The equations represent lines or planes that are parallel and distinct. They never cross, meaning no point satisfies all equations simultaneously.

1 Solution
✕

Consistent & Unique

The lines or planes intersect at exactly one single point. This point is the unique solution to the system.

∞ Solutions
⧉

Consistent & Dependent

The equations represent the exact same line or intersect along a common line/plane. Any point on that line is a valid solution.

2 Augmented Matrices & Row Operations

To solve large systems efficiently, we extract the coefficients and constants into an Augmented Matrix. We then use three Elementary Row Operations to simplify it: scaling a row, swapping rows, or adding a multiple of one row to another.
